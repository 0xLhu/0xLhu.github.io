I work in cyber threat intelligence. This blog is where I write the things I never get around to formalising elsewhere.

## What you will find here

Three recurring themes.

**The junction between CTI and detection.** How a threat intelligence report becomes a deployed detection rule, what gets lost along the way, and why that translation remains largely artisanal.

**Personal research.** Chiefly MalwareDNA, an attempt to apply computational phylogenetics to binary similarity. Positive and negative results, published either way.

**Questions of method.** How to assess a CTI capability, how to choose sources, how to measure without telling yourself stories.

## What you will not find here

No data from my professional work. No client telemetry, no real incident, no metric drawn from a production environment. What I write here starts from public sources, accessible corpora and my own reasoning.

Views expressed are my own.

## Writing, being wrong, correcting

I write partly in order to be corrected. Several of the projects published here are first attempts, with the flaws that implies. When I find an error after publication, I correct it and say so in the article rather than rewriting history.

If you spot a flaw in the reasoning, a misused source or a conclusion drawn too fast, write to me. That is the best use you can make of this blog.
